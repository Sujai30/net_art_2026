ONLINE EXHIBITION // EARLY WEB INTERACTION

PARTICIPATION AS MEDIUM

Curated by Sujai Reddy

This exhibition brings together three net artworks from the early years of the public web that treat user interaction as part of the artwork itself. Instead of offering a fixed image or message, these works depend on clicking, contributing, and navigating in order to fully exist. Douglas Davis’s The World’s First Collaborative Sentence turns writing into a collective, never-ending artwork shaped by its users. Martine Neddam’s Mouchette uses links and exploration to let viewers piece together a fictional online identity. Diller + Scofidio’s Refresh asks viewers to interpret a mix of live webcam imagery and staged narrative. Together, these works show how early net art challenged traditional ideas of authorship and spectatorship by making participation part of the medium itself.

NET ARTWORK 01

The World’s First Collaborative Sentence

Douglas Davis // 1994

An interactive online artwork in which users contribute to a continuously growing sentence. Because anyone can add to it, the work never has a fixed ending and exists through collective participation.

NET ARTWORK 02

Mouchette

Martine Neddam // 1996

An interactive website presented as the online identity of a fictional teenage girl. Users move through the work by clicking links and exploring pages, building their own sense of the character and narrative through navigation.

ENTER WORK

NET ARTWORK 03

Refresh

Diller + Scofidio // 1998

A web-based artwork that combines live webcam feeds with staged images and fictional narratives. By asking viewers to compare, interpret, and question what they see, the piece makes participation part of how the work is understood.
