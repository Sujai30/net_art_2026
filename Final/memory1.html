Memory: The Call

The pill dissolves like chalk on my tongue. The corridor tears open and I am in my own kitchen, leaning against the counter with a phone pressed to my ear. My sister’s voice crackles through the line, bright with life.

I tell her I needed air. I do not mention the anonymous message on my phone that told me to meet someone at the bus stop. I listen to her tell me about leftovers and weekend plans. We talk around what matters and fill the call with small things.

Even then, I feel that someone else is listening. There is a second breath on the line, slow and measured. I hang up first. I put on my coat and step out into the rain.
