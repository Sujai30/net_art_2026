Memory: The Bedside

I float above a hospital bed and watch myself sleep. Machines whisper in code around me. My chest rises and falls without my help. Tubes run into veins I no longer feel.

My sister sits beside the bed, fingers laced with mine. She tells me a story about when we were six, her voice breaking on my name. My mother kneads my hand like dough, her eyes closed, as if she can braid life back into me. My father stands by the window, his jaw clenched, staring at the parking lot lights. His silence is a wall I cannot climb.

I try to speak but I have no throat. I try to move but I am only light. I want to say I'm sorry. I'm not sorry I tried to expose the theft, but I am sorry I am leaving them with my unfinished sentences.
