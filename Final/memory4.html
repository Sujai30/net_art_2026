Memory: The Ledger

I am sitting at my desk on a late afternoon. Sunlight slants across piles of receipts and ledger books. Numbers blur together until a pattern snaps into focus. Someone has been siphoning money from the housing fund—small amounts at first, then larger.

I trace names and transfers. The name at the end of the chain is one that makes my stomach drop. A neighbour. A friend. I stare at the total for a long time. I pick up my phone and type: “I know what you did.” I hesitate before pressing send. Then I press it anyway.

My phone vibrates almost immediately. “Meet me where the buses stop running,” the reply says. There is a smiley face at the end, jaunty and cruel. I close my eyes and open them again. The sunlight has shifted. I hear the bus brakes hiss outside. My fate is sealed by a simple choice: to speak or to stay silent.
