After The Echo

I leave the half‑open door closed. A sterile smell leaks out—a mix of bleach and something sweet. I keep my eyes on the corridor ahead and try not to think about the machines I half remember. My hand brushes against the pill bottle. I still have choices.

As I walk, the sound of my footsteps changes. The floor seems smoother, the air less oppressive. Maybe refusing a memory preserves a mystery, or maybe it simply delays an inevitability.
