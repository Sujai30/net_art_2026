Memory: The Garden

The last pill tastes sweet. I find myself standing barefoot in a garden at dawn. Dew beads on the grass. The air is cold but the light is warm, a soft gold that makes everything look forgiving.

I hear my sister’s laugh somewhere behind me and the murmur of my mother kneading dough. I smell my father’s shaving cream and the rain on the alley. All my memories blur together here, not as fragments but as a single feeling: belonging.

I realise that knowledge was never the point. None of the pills change the fact of my death. What they change is the quality of my goodbye. In this garden, I am no longer afraid. I am grateful—for fries and phone calls and ledgers and letters, for pain and love. I kneel and let the dew soak through me. I let myself say yes to whatever is next.
