Memory: The Letter

The pill pulls a waxed envelope into focus. My name is written on it in careful script. I break the seal with trembling hands. Inside is a letter from the man who killed me.

He writes that he did not mean to end my life. He writes about the debts he owed and the pressure he was under. He says he admired my courage. He says he is sorry, not just for killing me but for betraying the community that trusted him.

Reading his words, anger and pity braid together. Part of me wants to crumple the letter and part of me wants to forgive him. Neither will change what happened. But understanding his desperation shifts something in me. I am still dead, but the sharpness of the wound dulls slightly. Forgiveness, I realise, is more for the dead than for the living.
