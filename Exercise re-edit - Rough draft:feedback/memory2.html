Memory: The Alley

The pill opens a door behind my eyes. Rain hits my face in stinging needles. I am running down an alley behind the closed pharmacy, shoes slapping against puddles. My chest is tight, but not from the run.

A hand grabs my jacket and yanks me backward. I don't see his face at first. I smell cigarettes and damp wool. My back hits the bricks. In the glint of a streetlamp I catch a silver ring.

Recognition is worse than impact. It is the face of a man I trusted to manage the funds of our community. His eyes are apologetic, almost tender. “You should have stayed out of it,” he says, and then there is only the sound of rain on plastic and the taste of metal.
